Dependent clauses come in a variety of forms that need to be handled differently depending on their relation to the main independent clause.

<!-- potentially put time here -->

# {{< heading "Reason" >}}
**から** attaches to both basic and polite predicates, but nouns and adjectival nouns are required to have the copula in between. Whatever から denotes as the reason has been interpreted so by the speaker, even if said reason wasn't responsible for the independent clause. It may alternatively be at the sentence end. In this case, if it is followed by the copula, a strong assertion is being made for the reason.

{{< subtext >}}
    ～のだから (or ～んだから) may be used when reasserting the reason or the speaker is being pushy.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「機械が壊れたからです。」"
    en="\"*It’s because the machine broke*.\""
%}}

**のだ** and んだ also does arguably stronger assertion. Remember that nouns and adjectival nouns require な in between. When asking "why," のかのですか or のかんですか are available. In casual speech, の alone performs the same thing. のだ will create a blunt question.

{{< subtext >}}
    Although the conjugations often occur in the predicate, the だ in のだ and んだ may conjugate to add nuance. Past is forceful and may translate to "it so happened that." Negative in questions conveys the speaker's desire for an answer. Negative-past can mean "it wasn't that."
{{< /subtext >}}

**ので** and **んで** is more objective than から. Additionally, the speaker has no control over the reason. When at the end of the sentence, the assertion is as strong as のだ but with a softer tone.

{{< subtext >}}
    ですので and ですんで appear frequently enough that they're correct. ますので and ますんで not so much. 
{{< /subtext >}}



# {{< heading "Although" >}}
**のに** may only be used when what is expected of the dependent clause does not actually play out. As a final particle, it is the sentence that does not result in an expected outcome.

<!-- uses grammar concepts not yet touched -->
{{< subtext >}}
    ～たらいい often attaches to the potential form and means "if only." Slap on のに after and some regret is infused into the meaning.

    ～ばいいのに, ～ばいいのだが, ばなあ, and ばねえ mean "although it would be nice if {{ subject }}..." It is a little cold with のに, though.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「食べないの? せっかく作ったのに。」"
    en="\"*You’re not going to eat it? Even though I went through all the trouble of making it*?\""
%}}
