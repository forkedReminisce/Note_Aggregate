# {{< heading "Reason" >}}
**から** denotes the dependent clause as the reason for the main clause. It attaches to either basic or polite predicates, with nouns and adjectival nouns taking the copula in between. The cause is perceived as such by the speaker, even if it isn't the reason in reality. It may alternatively be at the sentence end. In this case, if it is then followed by the copula, a strong assertion is being made.

{{< subtext >}}
    ～のだから (or ～んだから) may be used when reasserting the reason or the speaker is being pushy.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「機械が壊れたからです。」"
    en="\"*It’s because the machine broke*.\""
%}}

**のだ** and んだ strongly asserts the cause. When asking for the reason, のかのですか or のかんですか can be used. In casual speech, の alone performs the same thing; のだ will create a blunt question.

{{< subtext >}}
    のだ and んだ may also strongly assert an exclamation or desire.

    Remember that nouns and adjectival nouns require な in between.
{{< /subtext >}}

**ので** and んで is more objective than から. Additionally, the speaker has no control over the reason. When at the end of the sentence, the assertion is as strong as のだ and んだ but with a softer tone.

{{< subtext >}}
    ですので and ですんで appear frequently enough that they're correct. ますので and ますんで not so much. 
{{< /subtext >}}



# {{< heading "\"Although\"" >}}
**のに** may only be used when what is expected of the dependent clause does not actually play out. It may also be used at sentence end.

<!-- uses grammar concepts not yet covered -->
{{< subtext >}}
    ～たらいい often attaches to the potential form to mean "if only." Attaching のに after adds some regret.

    ～ばいいのに, ～ばいいのだが, ～ばなあ, and ～ばねえ mean "although it would be nice if..." ～ばいいのに is a little cold, though.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「食べないの? せっかく作ったのに。」"
    en="\"*You’re not going to eat it? Even though I went through all the trouble of making it*?\""
%}}

# {{< heading "Time" >}}
時 defines a moment for the main clause to occur in. This is best expressed with に. 頃 is a vaguer version of 時. 

{{< subtext >}}
    ～どき is a suffix that translates as "the best time."

    ～ごろ (頃) attaches to time to add "approximately." ～くらいに is another option. Alternatively, ～ごろ can mirror ～どき by attaching to the continuative form of some verbs.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「**ソウルの空港に着いたときに**、電話します。」"
    en="\"*I'll call you **when I have arrived at the airport in Seoul***.\""
%}}

前 places the dependent clause after the main clause in the timeline. Confusingly, the dependent clause is in non-past tense. までに is very similar to 前, but it does emphasize completing the main clause by the time of the dependent clause. "Right before" can be achieved with 直前, which can be used temporally or spatially. Spatially, の is a must.

{{% example_jp
    jp="「**爆発直前に**ガソリンが気化したような臭いがしていた。」"
    en="\"*There was a smell of gasoline vaporizing **right before the explosion***.\""
%}}

後 is flipped compared to 前. First, its spatial definition refers to the direction of the 後ろ or the remaining portion of a matter. It's temporal function behaves differently depending on the predicate of the dependent clause. Verb predicates use あと and must be in past tense. When no particle follows it, the main clause happens after the dependent clause. に adds that the main clause is immediately after, and it may be combined as には to emphasize or にも to add "even." Without に, から notes that the main clause is unexpected, whereas で highlights that the main clause is not immediately after the dependent clause finishing. The auxiliary ～てから also exists, but only for verbs with volition, allowing the dependent clause to establish the starting point for the main clause. For temporal or action-related nouns, ～ごに (or ～のごに for temporal nouns) exists. 

{{< subtext >}}
    ～てから allows the dependent clause to not have started yet, which あと cannot do.

    ～てから may be followed by other particles like は or の. It may even appear in the main clause when followed by the copula.
{{< /subtext >}}

間 fundamentally means "between." This makes sense spatially, where it may also take on the definition "among;" temporally, the dependent clause and main clause happen simultaneously. The dependent clause is usually in ～ている form, but it may appear as ～ていた form to indicate the recollection. With instantaneous verbs, the meaning that the dependent clause had been completed while the main clause begins is created, but a non-past conjugation will restore the simultaneous meaning. 間 may also be modified like a noun to produce time phrases that the main clause can occur during. に suggests that the dependent and main clauses finish at the same time, while には makes it so that the main clause in its entirety happens within the dependent clause.

{{< subtext >}}
    If the agent is the same between clauses, ～ている can be replaced with ～ながら.

    The main clause determines the tense of the sentence.

    ～ない is used, not ～ていない.
{{< /subtext >}}

内 is similar to 間. As a spatial noun, it can refer to the within or a place one belongs (e.g., club or workplace). The differences compared to 間 become more apparent in the temporal sense, with the main clause having to finish by the time the dependent clause finishes. The dependent clause is not usually in non-past tense—usually in ～ている—but it may be if it ends with the main clause. に is the most common particle, but は is possible.

{{< subtext >}}
    It is written 内 when used spatially, and うち for temporally.

    間 is not valid when the dependent clause is in non-past tense and it finishes when the main clause finishes because 間 describes when the dependent clause begins and ends. 

    間 and うち are both valid if the dependent clause just has a chance of temporarily pausing instead of finishing, with the latter being used if the pause primarily comes from the main clause starting.

    The particle が may also appear, restricting the main clause not to an action that must be completed before the dependent clause, but rather that it is a state that must remain true while the dependent clause is true.
{{< /subtext >}}
