Asking a *binary question* is very simple. In the *basic register*, a raised intonation or ？ is used. The *polite register* has the final particle **か**, attached directly onto the politeness marker. 

{{< subtext >}}
    Basic questions can use か, but if the copula verb is present, it must be dropped. If directed at someone, this question will have a brutish tone.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「大丈夫か?」"
    en="\"*You alright?*\""
%}}

Binary questions with a *negative predicate* are not actually negative. In fact, the speaker believes that they know the answer is affirmative. ==To ask about the negative, insert ん or の before the politeness marker==, if present anyway. On another note, じゃない may have ん～ or の～ precede it entirely. The question will remain affirmative, but some uncertainty is introduced.

{{< subtext >}}
    For verbs, ～ませんか is often used to invite someone to do something. 

    Basic binary questions that use ～の without か have a serious yet soft tone.

    ～のですか or ～んですか also convey the speaker's curiosity. ～ん is a contraction of ～の, especially in spoken speech. If a noun or adjectival noun precede it, insert な in between.

    じゃないか can be simplified to じゃんか. Without か creates an affirmative *statement* with a tone softener.
{{< /subtext >}}

Interrogatives can be used to create more useful questions. The appropriate interrogative for a question can differ from English's depending on how the topic is interpreted. As for where they go, they could be placed at the end with the copula verb or serve as the subject. The best way to differentiate between these two methods is to think of the interrogative as an entity when it's the subject; when it's in the predicate, it's just serving a grammatical function. This is thanks to them being nouns. In fact, they're adverbial nouns. This allows them to be used with other case particles or behave as adverbs.

{{< subtext >}}
    The basic register does not use the copula verb unless the intention is a stern tone.

    なぜ is actually just an adverb. If it is in the predicate, a noun must precede it. If that "noun" is a full phrase, put の in between.

    The final particle よ makes the question more expressive.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「誰が社長ですか。」"
    en="\"*Who is the company president*?\""
%}}



# {{< heading "Embedded Questions" >}}
Instead of directly modifying like a subordinate clause, embedded questions get ～か before attaching to the main clause. The sentence as a whole does not necessarily need to be a question, but there is an uncertainty with the "topic" of the embedded question—which is marked with が. Embedded questions can be modified by adverbial quantifiers or case particles. 

{{% example_jp
    jp="「**どの宿題が難しかったか**教えてください。」"
    en="\"*Please tell me **which homework was difficult***.\""
%}}

There's some variations of ～か that increase the uncertainty. By increasing order:
- ～か: no concern
- ～のか: the situation is at least bothersome 
- ～だか 
- ～んだか requiring nouns and adjectival nouns to modify per usual and adjectives and verbs to no longer modify.

{{< subtext >}}
    ～だか does not require な from nouns and adjectival nouns, but does require ～ん or ～の for adjectives and verbs. ～のか and ～んだか are per usual with nouns and adjectival nouns, though.
    
    ～だか may alternatively be used encourage an answer from the listener.
{{< /subtext >}}

～かどうか is a variation of ～か that means "whether or not." For all intents and purposes, it's not that different from ～か. In fact, どう is just a substitute for the inverse. This opens the door for multiple ～か marking each option the speaker is considering. The final ～か must be omitted if there is no question, unless the options are situations or actions, then the final ～か is necessary. ～のか, ～だか, and ～んだか may be used to convey greater indecisiveness or uncertainty, but ～んだか must not be used for trivial decisions.
