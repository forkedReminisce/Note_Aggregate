Politics is the use of authority to allocate scare resources and settle conflicts. *Authority* is the capacity to direct the behavior of others. This comes in the form of **legitimate coercion**. 

Coercion is necessary to uphold the political order. Domestically, a monopoly on coercion is crucial to dispelling rebels. There are even greater implications on the international level. With the ability to threaten war, states can discourage other states from messing with them. Additionally, military power helps determine every state's place in the international system.

However, granting political organizations the capacity for coercion comes with the risk of it being used for predation. This is known as the *coercion dilemma*. Democracy solves this issue on the domestic front, while the international system requires retaliatory military force. If this military force needs to be maintained after the conflict, it can be legitimated by members recognizing a continued threat, approval through democracy, and consulting multilateral alliances.



# {{< heading "War" >}}
War is costly. Borders change and there are refugee flows, which end up altering the demographic composition of the state. More horrifically, people die and the economy gets destroyed.

The external security threats of war can create an opportunity for the government to expand. Raising taxes, expanding the bureaucracy, and heightening restrictions on liberties; citizens willingly gives these up for security. However, there's always the flip side. The longer the war goes on, the more frustrated citizens will be of the costs. This leads to a rise in opposition, which can sometimes result in revolution.
