Auxiliary verbs are entirely separate from their standalone counterparts. They attach to predicate verbs in て-form of the final clause.



# {{< heading "Commands" >}}
Instructing someone to do something makes use of **auxiliary verbs meaning "to give."** Negative verbs are conjugated with ～ないで.  

*Basic* commands are just the て-form by itself. Attach ～くれません, ～くれます, or ～ください for *polite* commands.

{{< subtext >}}
    Styling the command as a question adds politeness.

    The final particle よ can be added to bring urgency. Alternatively, や gives off a scolding tone. 
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「速くしてよ！」"
    en="\"*Do it faster already*!\""
%}}



# {{< heading "States" >}}
Although **～いる** does not share definitions with いる, ==it does possess its conjugations==. Applicable parts of speech are the coupla verb and verbs. *Tenses, politeness, and negativity* are handled by this auxiliary verb.

<!-- TODO: ～てて is for conjunctive or command purposes ? -->
{{< subtext >}}
    ～いる does possess an attributive form.

    ～ている is commonly contracted without the い, even in polite speech.

    ～ていて can be contracted to ～てて.

    Instead of ～ていない, ～ないでいる or ～ずにいる are also valid. 
{{< /subtext >}}

～いる can note that the action is a routine. Or maybe one is telling a history of doing the action.

{{% example_jp
    jp="「アレックスさんはもう10回も日本に旅行していますよ。」"
    en="\"*Alex has already traveled to Japan 10 times*.\""
%}}

If the *verb takes time to complete*, ～いる notes that it is in progress. Progressive perfect tenses (e.g., "had been {{action}}ing") are also covered under this umbrella.

{{% example_jp
    jp="「お風呂はもう**沸いています**か。」"
    en="\"*Is the bath **hot** yet*?\""
%}}

If the *verb happens in an instant and affects the subject's state*, ～いる declares that this new state is persisting since said action. 

{{< subtext >}}
    Verbs in past conjugation do not say anything about whether the state is still there or not.

    ある and いる are prohibited verbs.

    Motion verbs are valid verbs. Using ～いる focuses exclusively on the arrival, and the subject is still at the destination. 

    Transitive verbs must not be able to be acted out by the subject.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「教師をしています。」"
    en="\"*I am a teacher*.\""
%}}

<!-- TODO: the direct object becomes the subject ? -->
**～ある** is an auxiliary verb that can replace ～いる in ==transitive predicates that have a different agent and direct object==—who is also the subject. ～ある *inexplicitly* mentions the existence of this agent. The agent is omitted, and the direct object is marked with が.

{{% example_jp
    jp="「彼の熱は計ってある。」"
    en="\"*His temperature was checked* **(by someone)**.\""
%}}
