Tension between the U.S. and Venezuela has existed for several decades, but it has escalated with the second Trump administration. It started with diplomatic threats and economic sanctions then American attacks against drug boats in the Caribbean. However, these attacks were performed in questionable legal circumstances. The justification of "disrupting drug trafficking" also opens the door for other opportunities outside of Venezuela. 

{{< subtext >}}
    Situation in Venezuela deemed a national security threat due to the number of American deaths due to drug overdose
{{< /subtext >}}

The U.S. painted Venezuela as complicit in drug trafficking, shaping the opinions of Americans and internationally.

After the U.S. captured Venezuelan President Maduro, other Latin American governments were antsy that they were next. Maduro's government was kept intact, though. This is corroborated by the fact that the Venezuelan vice-president was selected to be the successor rather than the opposition leader. "Donroe Doctrine" aims to reestablish the Monroe Doctrine by building a U.S. sphere of influence in the Americas.

Approval of Congress was not obtained before removing President Maduro. 

Venezuela has enormous untapped oil reserves. Controlling this oil makes the U.S a dominant global supplier of energy, decreases the influence of other major oil producers and make big consumers of oil more dependent on the U.S. Global oil supply can increase, bringing down the price of gasoline and tackling President Trump's biggest domestic problem of inflation and affordability. However, there are risks. It is expensive to rebuild the neglected infrastructure. Military presence is necessary to make sure the oil can be invested and extracted at scale. This could result in American casualties. Also ethical problems.

That is not to say that that Maduro was a good leader, in fact, he was corrupt. However, Venezuela will not turn around by taking the leader. Institutional changes and economic reform need to happen. This also does open the door for Venezuelan civil war, potentially bringing in Venezuelan refugees into the U.S., counteracting Trump's anti-immigration policy. 

A new level of aggression has been internationally set, potentially justifying China in a potential invasion of Taiwan. The international system against military force in foreign countries, that the U.S. built, is also shaken up. If the Venezuelan situation goes really bad, President Trump, his administration, and the Republican Party will be held responsible.
