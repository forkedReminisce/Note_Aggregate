Although tariffs are paid by the importer, it's often passed onto consumers. However, they do bring government revenue, protect domestic business, protect domestic jobs, encourage foreign companies to build domestic factories, and can be used as bargaining leverage.

{{< subtext >}}
    Domestic jobs are protected by helping local companies stay profitable, reducing the pressure to cut wages or move production overseas.
{{< /subtext >}}

Unfortunately, though, not only do tariffs raise the price of foreign goods, but domestic producers can raise their own prices out of greed. Choices are also reduced as foreign producers export less and retailers import less. Downstream industries will cut back on jobs because they cannot pass the full tariff of their inputs to consumers. Finally, retaliatory tariffs hurt domestic exporters. 



# {{< heading "Trump" >}}
Tariffs had always been low after WWII in support of free trade. President Trump broke this streak by raising them dramatically. He's using tariffs as a revenue tool and to obtain concessions from other states. He also claimed that he could impose tariffs without Congressional approval.

The Supreme Court ruled against Trump, which is significant because this Supreme Court has been quite friendly towards Trump. It specifically nullified tariffs using the *International Economic Emergency Powers Act* (IEEPA), which made up 75% of tariff revenue. IEEPA does not explicitly mention tariffs, let alone give presidents the power to levy them. The ruling was rooted on the fact that Congress processes the constitutional power to enact tariffs, and that if Congress wants to lend this power to the president, it must do so clearly.

Trump responded with a sweeping tariff using the Trade Act of 1974. This act is more limited than IEEPA; it is only effective for 150 days unless extended by Congress and it must be non-discriminatory. This hurts the trading partners who made significant concessions for lower tariff rates under IEEPA but now get higher tariffs. There are other avenues Trump could take to levy tariffs, but they are more difficult and slower than IEEPA. Additionally, the utility of tariffs is a faction of what Trump intended.
