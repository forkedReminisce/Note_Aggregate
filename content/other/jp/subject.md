**が** is a *case particle* that marks the **subject of the sentence**. Explicit use of it typically signifies that *the case is unfamiliar to the listener*. Multiple が creates a nested structure that expands out from the farthest right. 

{{< subtext >}}
    Neutrality depends on more factors than just sentence structure.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp 
    jp="「この魚のほうが**歯応えが弱い**。」"
    en="\"This fish is **whose texture is weaker**."
%}}



Beyond neutral sentences, が ***gains* a function of exhaustive listing**. This means that ==*the speaker's* other options do not satisfy the sentence==: exclusivity. 

{{< subtext >}}
    To make the exclusivity clear to the listener, append ～のほう to the subject.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp 
    jp="「波**が**高っ！」"
    en="\"*These waves* **(compared to those I've seen)** *are high!*\""
%}}
