All pure adjectives end in ～い. It is this that gets dropped in conjugations. Adjectives possess an attributive and predicative form.

# {{< heading "Polite" >}}
These conjugations do not have an attributive form.

{{% table_conj
    non_past="～いです"
    past="～かったです"
    neg="～くありません ～くないです"
    neg_past="～くありませんでした ～くなかったです"
%}}

{{< subtext >}}
    Negative and negative-past conjugations can have は inserted after the く for a contrast effect on the adjective.
{{< /subtext >}}



# {{< heading "Akin" >}}
Adjectival nouns are another way to modify a noun. *Not all adjectival nouns are nouns*. To commence the modification, ==attach the appropriate copula verb to the adjectival noun==. This may be performed in attributive or predicative form. The only exception is ==basic, non-past attributive==, where ==な== prevails over だ.  

An outright noun that modifies uses ==の==. When the modified noun is a relative noun (e.g., 下), it becomes an adverb. 

<!-- why is の sometimes omitted??? -->
{{< subtext >}}
    The modifier noun may have attributes of its own, whether it be a pure adjective or even another noun using の.

    の is sometimes omitted.
{{< /subtext >}}
