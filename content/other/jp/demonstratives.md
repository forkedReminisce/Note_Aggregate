**Demonstratives** can be used as a pronoun or attributively. There are different sets when referring to *entities* or *locations*, but first morae is always either こ, そ, or あ depending on *distance*.



# {{< heading "Entities" >}}
This set of demonstratives correspond to the words "**this**" and "**that**." Before listing them, it is important to note the physicality or current presence of the entity will alter the condition required for a particular demonstrative. ==Don't interpret the entity too literally and think about what it is being particularly addressed==. 

- **これ**: the entity is close to or aware by the speaker (sometimes the listeners too)
- **それ**: physically close to the listener (but not the speaker) or aware *strictly* by either the speaker or listener
- **あれ**: far from the speaker and listener or aware by both

{{< subtext >}}
    When using の with these demonstratives as the modifier noun, the modified noun must be one that cannot accept "the" when translated *by itself*.

    ～れは can be contracted to either ～りゃ or ～ら, with the latter carrying an angry tone.

    Replacing ～れ with ～いつ is a very casual way of referring specifically to people.
{{< /subtext >}}

These *pronouns* already have a plural function. However, attaching ～ら can highlight each item's individuality. この, その, あの are the standard *attributive forms*. With ～ら is like これらの….

{{< subtext >}}
    あれら is most particularly used to declare that the listener is also aware of the entity.
    
    When trying to nominalize an area around the entity (e.g., "inside of it"), use the attributive forms.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「その学生さんは誰ですか。」"
    en="\"*Who is that student*?\""
%}}



# {{< heading "Locations" >}}
"**Here**" and "**there**" can refer not only to physical places but *also situations and amounts* (e.g., "that extent"). Just like the entities set of demonstratives, replacing ～こ with ～の generates the *attributive forms*.

- **ここ**: close to the speaker (and likely listeners too)
- **そこ**: close to only the listener or *more* aware by either the speaker or listener
- **あそこ**: far from the speaker and listener or acutely aware by both

{{< subtext >}}
    Appending ～ら辺 onto the nominal form serves to mean "around (place)."
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「**そこのお姉さん**、財布を落としましたよ。」"
    en="\"***Miss there**, you dropped your wallet*.\""
%}}



# {{< heading "Polite" >}}
こちら is the politer form of これ *and* ここ. It may also serve as a first person or third person pronoun. As a first person pronoun, it is indirect. The third person pronoun implies that "this person" is at least of equal status. 

そちら covers それ and そこ. As a pronoun, it could serve as a respectful "you." Alternatively, it may mean "that person" if the listener is closer to them, whether it be in proximity or relationship.

あちら gets あれ and あそこ. It can also be a third person pronoun with the same restrictions as its demonstratives.

These polite forms may be contracted as こっち, そっち, and あっち. They function the same but are obviously more casual as contractions. Additionally, あちこち means "here and there."



# {{< heading "Likeness And Degree" >}}
<!-- goes before a noun, like この -->
There are demonstratives which cover means like "like this." They are こんな, そんな, and あんな. They have the same restrictions as the demonstratives previous. Additionally, there is a どんな for a "what sort of." Actually, these are the casual forms; the formal ones are このような, そのような, あのような, and どのような.

{{% example_jp
    jp="「公園には**そのような小鳥**がたくさんいます。」"
    en="\"*There are many **such birds** in the park*.\""
%}}

These words can sometimes be adjectival nouns, but it is uncommon. Really, the only time it comes up is when it is part of a subordinate clause that is followed by の. 

{{% example_jp
    jp="「事態があんななので、どうすることもできない。」"
    en="\"*Since the situation is like that, there is nothing that can be done about it*.\""
%}}

There's also some additional forms. For brevity, only one version will be used as an example, but the others can be easily constructed. こういう風 and こんな風 are adjectival nouns that emphasize the likeness; こんな and the like focuses on the "this" and its degree. These adjectival nouns nowadays go with な, but they used to be with の. この風 is also possible but more direct.

{{< subtext >}}
    ああいう風 is the exception to the construction. It is the exception for こう～.
{{< /subtext >}}

こうした and こうしたものだ differ from the previous by making the reference an ongoing thing (e.g., an ongoing situation). The latter may only be used at sentence end. した may be replaced with いう or いった. いう kind of sweepingly relates something, so いった allows for fine-tuning by specifying what the thing something is like is.

{{< subtext >}}
    どうしたものか is the exception to the construction. 
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「キャンディーやアイスクリーム、こういった甘いものが好きです。」"
    en="\"*I like sweet things **of the candy and ice cream varieties***.\""
%}}

There are also adverbial forms. こんなに refers to degree. In casual speech, the に may be dropped. こう, このように, こういう風に, こうして, and こうやって instead focus on likeness. ～やって is more casual than ～して.
