Iran has strategic importance due to its geographic placement: it's in the middle between the Middle East, Central Asia, and South Asia. It is also near the Strait of Hormuz, a key choke point of oil shipping. Iran is also near the top in oil and natural gas reserves. Iran has a Shia Muslim majority. Iran is a theocratic dictatorship, but it has elections albeit suspicious. Iran has a lot of regional enemies, but their allies primarily consist of armed militant groups (e.g., Hamas).

{{< subtext >}}
    Iran gets around international sanctions on oil by selling most of its oil to China... at a cheaper price. This increases its economic vulnerability.
{{< /subtext >}}

Iran was actually a key ally to the U.S. until the Iranian Revolution. Relatively recently, the Iran Nuclear Deal was reached, but Trump withdrew from it. Relations worsened with the U.S. siding with Israel in a war against Iran. 

The current crisis is centered on two issues. Iran is violently suppressing protests, and President Trump has threatened military force, even going as far as performing demonstrations of force. The second issue is a new Iran Nuclear Deal. The Israeli Prime Minister and Trump want it to also include limits on ballistic missiles and regional proxies, and Trump will resort force if necessary. 

Trump sees force as a potential solution because Iran is politically, economically, and militarily vulnerable. Politically, it is experiencing the largest protests. Iran is using an unprecedented level of coercion. Economically, hyperinflation and poor provision of necessities not only fuel dissent but also increase the pressure of U.S. sanctions. That way, the U.S. can taunt Iran with cutting sanctions. Militarily, Iran suffered multiple military setbacks. Israel weakened Iran's proxies and bypassed Iran's air defenses to destroy nuclear and ballistic weapons facilities. This encourages the U.S. to carry through with force since it seems like Iran doesn't have the capabilities to retaliate. 

In talks for a new Iran Nuclear Deal, the U.S.' position is zero enrichment of uranium and expanded demands in restrictions on ballistic missiles and proxies. Iran rejects all of this, but is willing to cooperate to a greater degree in terms of getting close to zero enrichment. 

U.S. threats of military force alone can get a lot of concessions. However, if the U.S. were to ever follow through, there are consequences. Iran can close the Strait of Hormuz, which disrupts global oil trade. Inflation in oil prices would, in turn, strike the U.S. back domestically. Iran could also attack the American assets in the Middle East. If the regime is defeated, its replacement could be worse and could have other international ramifications.
