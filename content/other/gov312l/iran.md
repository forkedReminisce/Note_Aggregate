Iran is near the top in oil and natural gas reserves while also possessing a lot of control over the Strait of Hormuz, a crucial choke point of oil shipping. It has a lot of regional enemies, but its allies are primarily proxies.

Iran was actually a key ally to the U.S. until the Iranian Revolution. Relatively recently, the Iran Nuclear Deal was reached, but President Trump withdrew from it in his first term. Relations worsened when Iran got into a war with Israel. Today, the current crisis with Iran stems from two issues: violent suppression of protects and a new Iran Nuclear Deal. 

One of Trump's potential solutions to the first problem is military force. Politically, Iran is experiencing the largest protests in its history and shutting it down with heavy violence. Economically, hyperinflation, a cause of this dissent, increases the pressure of U.S. sanctions. Militarily, Israel has weakened Iran's proxies and bypassed its air defenses, potentially leaving Iran unable to retaliate an attack.

Trump's goals with a new Iran Nuclear Deal is zero enrichment of uranium and limits on ballistic missiles and proxies. Iran rejected, but it is willing to get close to zero enrichment. Alternatively, the U.S. can turn to war. However, Iran can close the Strait of Hormuz. This would inflate oil prices, which can worsen the U.S.' inflation. Other consequences are Iran attacking American assets in the Middle East and, if the current regime is defeated, a potentially worse replacement.



# {{< heading "War" >}}
The conflict is that the U.S. does not approve of Iran's proxies and nuclear program. A war for Iran could lead to collapse, and while not existential, the U.S. could indirectly disrupt the export of oil in the Middle East. Private information held by Iran is that they preferred to go to war rather than make extensive concessions. Likewise, the U.S. thought it would win a war quickly and it was being pressured by Israel and Saudi Arabia that the solution would be more permanent than diplomacy. 

Stopping Iran from producing a nuclear weapon is challenging. Strikes don't eliminate the knowledge on how to build such weapons, stripping Iran of its highly enriched uranium stockpile is immensely difficult, and such intervention actually strengthens Iran's need for nuclear deterrence. 

Iran's main goal is for the current regime to survive. To survive against the stronger forces of the U.S. and Israel, asymmetrical warfare only requires inflicting a cheap high cost on the opponents to pressure them to stop. Additionally, by attacking neighbors allied with the U.S., they can put extra pressure on the U.S. Iran has also closed the Strait of Hormuz, increasing global oil prices. 

Currently, the U.S.' biggest priority is reopening the Strait of Hormuz. However, options are limited, risky, and slow. Kharg Island, Iran's vital oil export hub, does seem like a viable option. Therefore, the U.S. destroyed military assets on the island but intentionally left oil infrastructure. If U.S. troops can then capture the island, that would grant a lot of leverage, but that would be difficult to achieve. 

The U.S. could just end its campaign and declare victory. This avoids a long war and *may* stop economic conditions from worsening. However, the current regime would be left in place and Iran can still continue with its nuclear program. Continuing the war can achieve the goals, but there would be more casualties, global economic conditions would worsen, and it would undermine the Republican Party.
