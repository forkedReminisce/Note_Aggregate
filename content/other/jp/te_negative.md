<!-- for the copula, is ～でなくて a completely fresh negative て-form -->
To generate the **basic negatives** of て-form, take the basic negative conjugation and replace ～ない with ～なくて or ～ないで. ==The negative does not have all the nuances of the [affirmative](/other/jp/te_affirmative "Conjoining Affirmative Clauses")==.

**～なくて** works with all parts of speech. Whether the clauses happen in order or simultaneously is up to interpretation. Maybe whatever *doesn't* happen in the first clause explains the second clause. ==～なくて is also used to generate a number of templates==:
- "{{subject}} is not {{attribute}}, but {{attribute}}"
- **～なくてよかった**: "glad that {{...}} did not happen"
- **～なくてはだめ, ～なくてはいけない, ～なくてはならない**: "must {{...}}," は is pronounced /wa/
<!-- covered in も section -->
- **～なくてもいい**: "it's OK not to {{...}}"

{{% example_jp
    jp="「 雨が降らなくてよかった。」"
    en="\"*I'm glad that it didn't rain*.\""
%}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「規則を守らなくてはいけない。」"
    en="\"*You must follow the rules*.\""
%}}

**～ないで** is exclusive to *verbs that require a degree of will*. Like ～なくて, ～ないで can relate two clauses. However, ～ないで will emphasize the volition in the inaction. Other nuances of ～ないで are to "mark" the means or method of the second clause, often implying that the inaction of the first clause is against expectation. In another case, if the subjects are different, the speaker finds the inaction against their expectation. "{{action}} instead of {{action}}" utilizes ～ないで as well.

{{% example_jp
    jp="「**お酒を飲まないで**、働いた。」"
    en="\"*I worked **instead of drinking alcohol***.\""
%}}
