Greenland has great potential. 
- Strategic position that makes it ideal for air defense against Russia 
- Sits at the center of the emerging Arctic shipping lanes, which are faster and safer to travel through to Asia
- Some of the world's largest deposits of rare earth minerals, challenging China's global dominance 

{{< subtext >}}
    Climate, geography, strict environmental regulations, and lack of infrastructure and people stand in the way of mining Greenland's rare earth minerals.
{{< /subtext >}}

Greenland has always played a significant role in U.S. defense since WWII. So important, in fact, that the U.S. had tried to buy it all those years ago. When wind hit that the USSR was developing nuclear bombs, the *1951 Defense of Greenland Agreement* was signed between the U.S. and Denmark to allow greater U.S. military presence on the territory. 

That is the setup of the Greenland crisis, and President Trump's crises follows a predictable structure. 
1. *Prelude*: Trump announces a provocative position that gets international attention
2. *Escalation*: make threats, which implies that the administration is willing to go further than expected, increases perceived urgency, and prompts responses from others
3. *Resolution*: suddenly step back and make a settlement, and the U.S. will claim it a success
4. *Fallout*: trust in the U.S. erodes and everyone has strategic doubt

The prelude started at the end of Trump's first term, but escalation only came in his second term. He refused to rule out the use of military force in gaining control over Greenland, then the U.S.' invasion of Venezuela (added with messaging implying Greenland was next) really woke European leaders up. European countries sent NATO military personnel to Greenland, and Trump threatened to place a tariff on them. 

Eventual meetings with Denmark and Greenland ended with Greenland wishing to stay with Denmark, making it clear to the U.S. that no one was going to budge. Additionally, the Republican party was starting to falter and the stock market plummeted. As a result, Trump walked back on the use of military force, and then tariffs after discussing a potential agreement with NATO on Greenland. 

The Greenland crisis has irreversibly changed the international order. The world has now entered a new period of great power conflict, where the great powers will use their capabilities to pursue their interests without any regard to international rules. It's time for middle powers to start taking responsibility for their security and prosperity. Middle powers must start building strength, developing resilient economies to reduce the leverage of great powers, diversify partnerships, and band together and act collectively.
