Asking a *binary question* is very simple. In the *basic register*, a raised intonation or ？ is used. The *polite register* has the final particle **か**, attached directly onto the politeness marker. 

{{< subtext >}}
    Basic questions can use か, but if the copula verb is present, it must be dropped. If directed at someone, this question will have a brutish tone.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「大丈夫か?」"
    en="\"*You alright?*\""
%}}

Binary questions with a *negative predicate* are not actually negative. In fact, the speaker believes that they know the answer is affirmative. ==To ask about the negative, insert の before the politeness marker==, if present anyway. On another note, じゃない may have の～ or ん～ precede it entirely. The question will remain affirmative, but some uncertainty is introduced.

{{< subtext >}}
    For verbs, ～ませんか is often used to invite someone to do something. 

    Basic binary questions that use ～の without か have a serious yet soft tone.
    
    ～のですか and ～んですか force verbs into their basic conjugations.

    Polite binary questions using ～のですか or ～んですか convey the speaker's curiosity.

    じゃないか can be simplified to じゃんか. Without か creates an affirmative *statement* with a tone softener.
{{< /subtext >}}



*Question words* can be used to create more useful questions. The first level has the word in the predicate followed by the copula verb. 

{{< subtext >}}
    The basic register does not use the copula verb unless the intention is a stern tone.

    何です is pronounced なんです.

    The final particle よ makes the question more expressive.
{{< /subtext >}}

To focus on an aspect of the topic, move the question word into the subject. In the case these two levels offer minimal difference from one another, the latter is more direct.

{{% example_jp
    jp="「座席はどこがいいですか。」"
    en="\"*What seats are good?*\""
%}}
