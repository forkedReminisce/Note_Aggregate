**Demonstratives** can be used as a pronoun or attributively. There are different sets when referring to *entities* or *locations*, but first morae is always either こ, そ, or あ depending on *distance*.



# {{< heading "Entities" >}}
This set of demonstratives correspond to the words "**this**" and "**that**." Before listing them, it is important to note the physicality or current presence of the entity will alter the condition required for a particular demonstrative. ==Don't interpret the entity too literally and think about what it is being particularly addressed==. 

- **これ**: the entity is close to or aware by the speaker (sometimes the listeners too)
- **それ**: physically close to the listener (but not the speaker) or aware *strictly* by either the speaker or listener
- **あれ**: far from the speaker and listener or aware by both

{{< subtext >}}
    When using の with these demonstratives as the modifier noun, the modified noun must be one that cannot accept "the" when translated *by itself*.

    ～れは can be contracted to either ～りゃ or ～ら, with the latter carrying an angry tone.

    Replacing ～れ with ～いつ is a very casual way of referring specifically to people.
{{< /subtext >}}

These *pronouns* already have a plural function. However, attaching ～ら can highlight each item's individuality. この, その, あの are the standard *attributive forms*. With ～ら is like これらの….

{{< subtext >}}
    あれら is most particularly used to declare that the listener is also aware of the entity.
    
    When trying to nominalize an area around the entity (e.g., "inside of it"), use the attributive forms.
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「その学生さんは誰ですか。」"
    en="\"*Who is that student*?\""
%}}



# {{< heading "Locations" >}}
"**Here**" and "**there**" can refer not only to physical places but *also situations and amounts* (e.g., "that extent"). Just like the entities set of demonstratives, replacing ～こ with ～の generates the *attributive forms*.

- **ここ**: close to the speaker (and likely listeners too)
- **そこ**: close to only the listener or *more* aware by either the speaker or listener
- **あそこ**: far from the speaker and listener or acutely aware by both

{{< subtext >}}
    Appending ～ら辺 onto the nominal form serves to mean "around (place)."
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「**そこのお姉さん**、財布を落としましたよ。」"
    en="\"***Miss there**, you dropped your wallet*.\""
%}}



# {{< heading "Likeness" >}}
For brevity, only one class will be listed at a time, but the others are easy to construct from there. Additionally, there is a class for asking what the entity relates to, and it starts with ど～.

**こんな** go before nouns to say it is "like this," particularly focusing on the degree both entities are at. The formal ones go このような. These words can sometimes be adjectival nouns, but it is uncommon. Really, the only time it comes up is when it is part of a subordinate clause that is followed by の. 

{{% example_jp
    jp="「公園には**そのような小鳥**がたくさんいます。」"
    en="\"*There are many **such birds** in the park*.\""
%}}

The basic adverbial form is **こう**. Several words build on top of this one, like このように, こういう風に, こうして, and こうやって. These adverbs plus このように refer to likeness. こんなに, another adverb, instead relates to degree.

{{< subtext >}}
    Instead of あう, it's ああ.

    こうやって is more casual than こうして.
{{< /subtext >}}

こうした and こうしたものだ makes *this* an ongoing thing. The latter may only be used at sentence end. した may be replaced with いう or いった. いう kind of sweepingly relates things, so いった allows for specifying what it is the entity something is narrowly like.

{{< subtext >}}
    どうしたものか is the exception to the construction. 
{{< /subtext >}}

{{% example_jp
    jp="「キャンディーやアイスクリーム、こういった甘いものが好きです。」"
    en="\"*I like sweet things **of the candy and ice cream varieties***.\""
%}}

こういう風 and こんな風 are adjectival nouns that emphasize the likeness. この風 is also possible but more direct.



# {{< heading "Polite And Pronouns" >}}
こちら is the politer form of これ *and* ここ. It may also serve as an indirect first person pronoun or a third person pronoun. The third person pronoun implies that "this person" is *at least* of equal status. 

そちら covers それ and そこ. As a pronoun, it could serve as a respectful second person pronoun. Alternatively, as a third person pronoun, it may mean "that person" if the listener is closer to them, whether it be in proximity or relationship.

あちら gets あれ and あそこ. It can also be a third person pronoun with the expected restrictions of あれ.

{{< subtext >}}
    The contractions こっち, そっち, and あっち exist, obviously with a casual twist.
{{< /subtext >}}
